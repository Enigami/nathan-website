Day 1: Homebrew on the SNES CD

The SNES CD. Most people know it as the "Nintendo PlayStation" that never was. A few prototypes exist, and almost no software was ever written for it.

A personal dream of mine for years has been to make homebrew games for it, and drawing on years of 65816 assembly and digging into the inner workings of SNES hardware, I'm working to turn this dream into reality.

The SFX-100 "Nintendo PlayStation"

In the late 80s, Nintendo worked with Sony on two projects. One was the sound chip for the Super Famicom (known in the west as the SNES). The other, starting development in 1988, was the PlayStation, a console that would be capable of running CD-based media while also retaining support for SNES cartridges. Licensing disagreements resulted in Nintendo breaking their partnership with Sony in 1991 and partnering with Philips to develop a CD-ROM add-on for the SNES instead. That project was ultimately canceled in 1993. However, one of the SFX-100 PlayStation prototypes created during Nintendo's and Sony's partnership surfaced in 2015 and was restored, and it can even run homebrew games developed for the console that never was.

The Plan

I've spent years tinkering with modifying games for retro hardware, primarily the NES, SNES and Game Boy. One of my dream projects has been to develop and release an original game for one of these systems. But the SNES CD in particular has always appealed to me. Partly for its practically limitless (by SNES standards) 700MB capacity for games, partly for the challenge, and partly for the joy of turning fantasy into reality. The SNES CD largely exists as an unobtainable prototype with very little in the way of development. There's just Martin Korth's no$sns emulator which emulates the SFX-100 and a couple of homebrew games released a decade ago. I want to change that.

I've got many games I want to create, and so developing an environment for NES and SNES to ease and accelerate their development has been a long ongoing project. It's finally coming together in a form built around intuitive memory management and advanced features, with an easy to use UI similar to GameMaker or NESmaker. Once it's functional enough, I plan to make it publicly available so anyone else wanting to develop games for the SNES CD can do so without doing everything from scratch.

One obstacle, though, is the BIOS. It's Sony's property, so it can’t legally be shared, which makes the SNES CD hard to open up to developers. One solution I've been developing is a clean room BIOS recreating the functionality of the prototype BIOS but not referencing any of Sony's code. So far, it is able to take the place of the original SFX-100 BIOS and interface with the emulated CD-ROM drive in no$sns. This way, there would be a free publicly available BIOS that projects such as conan_the_great's physical SFX-100 recreation could use. I'm planning to add extra optional features beyond that of the original BIOS, namely drivers for MSU-1 that would enable playing SNES CD games on SD2SNES & FXPak Pro flash cartridges, a runtime library taking advantage of unused cartridge space, and implementing the SFX-100's planned SuperFX enhancement chip support that isn't present in the prototype. Developers could choose to take advantage of these features or ignore them to retain full prototype compatibility, or even design games to be able to support both.

With my IDE and BIOS coming along, I've also started work on my first game for the SNES CD.

What's done

  • Enough of the IDE to be useful for my game's development.
  • BIOS passing initial SFX-100 start-up tests with no$sns.
  • A raycasting engine for a stock SNES with a couple test levels & full game design document.

There's a lot of work still ahead, but the project has been mapped out and now it's just a matter of building it brick by brick.

What comes next

Custom builds of Mesen and SD2SNES/FXPak Pro supporting the BIOS cartridge mapper via MSU-1 are the current priority so I can test on those. They are near completion and will be ready soon. Following that, I will continue development of the IDE, BIOS and my first game for the SNES CD. If any of this interests you, I would love to hear from you. About

I am Nathaniel Harris, a systems software engineer. I build reliable software for complex systems.

I am self-taught, and I have done this work for more than fifteen years, from custom voxel game engines to space-domain awareness tools. Today I work on Forerunner at Space Interactions, on the services and pipelines behind satellite and orbital data, and the views that show it.

I work at every layer: Rust and MongoDB services, TypeScript and Electron frontends, C++ and HLSL on the GPU, and assembly on the bare machine. I learned all of it by hand, years before AI coding tools. Now I use agents like Claude Code to move faster, and fifteen years of doing the work myself is how I know when their output is right. I do my best work on small teams that build systems which seem impossible at that size.

I like domains where the rules are external and non-negotiable, such as orbital data, field-service operations and SMS compliance. To learn an unfamiliar system, I take it apart until its opinions become obvious. Then I work inside it, not against it.

Based in Interlochen, Michigan. Co-founder of Harris XR.
